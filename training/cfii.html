CFII Study Guide

A CFII lesson has to do more than get the airplane through an approach. The learner needs to understand the setup, recognize when something is wrong, manage workload, and know which source controls the procedure.

Subjects worth being able to teach cold

  • Instrument privileges, currency, IPCs, instructor privileges, endorsements, and recordkeeping that apply to the lesson.
  • Instrument scan, attitude control, trim, spatial disorientation, and the errors that show up when workload rises.
  • IFR weather, icing, convection, alternates, fuel, and how to teach a real go/change-the-plan decision.
  • Navigation equipment and automation: source selection, modes, database/procedure setup, failure indications, and backups.
  • Clearances, departures, enroute navigation, holding, arrivals, approaches, missed approaches, and circling when applicable.
  • Partial-panel work, electrical or display failures, lost communications, automation surprises, and workload management.

Use one flight instead of six disconnected lessons

Pick an IFR route and make the learner plan it. Change one thing at a time: lower ceilings, stronger winds, an inoperative unit, a reroute, a hold, or a missed approach. Ask what changed, what did not, and where they would verify the answer. That exposes weak understanding much faster than another list of definitions.

Use these sources first

Use the current FAA practical-test standard that applies to the instructor rating and task, current 14 CFR, the AIM, the Instrument Flying Handbook, the Aviation Instructor’s Handbook, and the exact aircraft and avionics documents. Procedure charts and weather must be current when you use them in a lesson.
