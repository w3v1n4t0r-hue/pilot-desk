Dynamic hydroplaning speed

Dynamic hydroplaning can occur when a tire rides on a layer of water instead of maintaining normal contact with the runway. Pilots are often taught a tire-pressure rule of thumb to estimate the speed at which dynamic hydroplaning can begin.

The common rule

A widely taught estimate is hydroplaning speed in knots ≈ 9 × the square root of tire pressure in PSI. It is a training estimate, not a precise boundary between safe and unsafe braking.

Worked example

For a tire pressure of 36 PSI, the square root is 6. Multiplying by 9 gives an estimated dynamic hydroplaning speed of about 54 knots.

Why it is only an estimate

Water depth, tire tread, runway texture, tire condition, inflation, braking technique and aircraft configuration all affect real hydroplaning behavior. Contaminated-runway performance is more complex than one equation.

Hydroplaning can continue below the onset estimate

The rule is usually taught as an approximate onset speed. Once hydroplaning begins, reduced tire contact and braking effectiveness may persist as the aircraft slows, depending on conditions.

Runway contamination matters

Standing water, slush and other contamination can affect acceleration, braking and directional control. Operational decisions should use current runway condition information, approved performance data and any applicable limitations.

Tire pressure must be correct

The equation depends directly on tire pressure. Incorrectly maintained tire pressure can affect both the estimate and tire performance in general. Use the aircraft maintenance or servicing information for correct inflation values.
