REFERENCE HUB

Aircraft performance and weight & balance reference

Aircraft performance is a system of connected variables rather than a collection of isolated formulas. Weight, temperature, pressure, wind, configuration, speed and center of gravity can all affect the final operational result. This hub groups PilotDesk's performance and loading guides around those relationships.

Atmosphere and performance

Pressure altitude, ISA temperature and density altitude explain the atmospheric inputs behind many performance charts.

Climb and obstacle math

Climb rate versus climb gradient explains FPM versus ft/NM. Obstacle climb gradient and flight-path angle expand that relationship.

Turns, stalls and maneuvering

Turn radius and rate, standard-rate turns and accelerated stall/load factor show how speed and bank are connected. Maneuvering speed at weight explains why Va decreases as aircraft weight decreases.

Glide and runway considerations

Glide range explains no-wind glide geometry. Dynamic hydroplaning speed explains the common tire-pressure rule of thumb and its limitations.

Weight and balance

Weight and balance basics, the loading envelope, useful load and payload, and moment and CG cover the core loading workflow.

CG movement

Percent MAC, ballast and CG, and CG shift with fuel burn explain how loading position changes across a flight.

Use the approved performance data

POH chart interpolation explains how to work between published chart values when the approved chart permits it. PilotDesk's POH Chart Studio is designed as a training aid, not a replacement for the current aircraft POH or AFM.
