FAA CERTIFICATES & RATINGS

Certificates, ratings, and the path between them

Use this as a plain-language map of the FAA certificate structure. A certificate describes a level of pilot or instructor privilege; category, class, instrument, and type ratings define where that privilege applies.

Technical basis — flight training reference

Primary technical context: the applicable FAA Airman Certification Standards, FAA handbooks, regulations, and current aircraft documents. Study material should explain the underlying relationship and source, not replace the controlling publication or aircraft-specific procedure.

FAA Airman Certification Standards · PilotDesk Sources & Methods

Study with the current rule. Eligibility, experience, and operating privileges come from current 14 CFR and the applicable FAA ACS. Confirm details with your instructor and the latest FAA publication.

Pilot certificates

Student pilot

Learn and log experience under an instructor’s supervision before exercising solo privileges.

Sport and recreational pilot

Limited privileges with eligibility and operating limits defined in Part 61.

Private pilot

General personal flying privileges, subject to the certificate’s category/class, endorsements, and operating rules.

Commercial pilot

Commercial privileges in the ratings held, with additional experience, knowledge, and skill standards.

Airline transport pilot

The highest pilot certificate, with category/class ratings and experience requirements set by regulation.

Ratings that attach to a certificate

Category and class

Airplane, rotorcraft, glider, lighter-than-air, powered lift, powered parachute, and weight-shift-control are categories. Examples include airplane single-engine land (ASEL), airplane multi-engine land (AMEL), single-engine sea, helicopter, and glider classes.

Instrument rating

Instrument airplane, helicopter, and powered-lift ratings authorize IFR operations within the applicable category and class. They do not replace currency, equipment, weather, or procedure requirements.

Type rating

A type rating authorizes operation of a specific aircraft type. The aircraft’s certification, weight, propulsion, and operating rules determine when one is required.

Instructor and ground instructor certificates

Flight instructor certificates include airplane single-engine, airplane multi-engine, instrument, helicopter, glider, and other category/class privileges as applicable. Ground instructor certificates include Basic (BGI), Advanced (AGI), and Instrument (IGI).

Official sources

  • FAA Airman Certification Standards — current practical-test standards.
  • 14 CFR Part 61 — certification, ratings, training, and experience.
  • 14 CFR Part 91 — general operating and flight rules.
  • FAA handbooks — knowledge and instructional references.
  • PilotDesk ACS and FAR study map — a task-oriented way to connect the standard to its source.
