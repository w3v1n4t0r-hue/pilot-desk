Reciprocal headings

A reciprocal heading points exactly 180 degrees opposite another heading or course. Pilots use reciprocals for runway directions, reverse courses, situational awareness, and quick mental math.

The 180-degree rule

If the original heading is less than 180°, add 180°. If it is 180° or greater, subtract 180°. A heading of 070° has a reciprocal of 250°. A heading of 310° has a reciprocal of 130°.

Runway numbers

Runway designators are based on magnetic direction rounded to the nearest ten degrees with the final zero removed. Opposite runway ends normally differ by 18, which mirrors the 180-degree reciprocal relationship.

Heading versus course

The math is the same whether you are reversing a heading, bearing, or course. The operational meaning is different, so keep track of whether the number describes where the airplane points or the path over the ground.

Common mistake

Do not let the answer exceed 360° or fall below 001° without wrapping it back into the normal compass range.
