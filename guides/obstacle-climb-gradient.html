Obstacle climb gradient explained

An obstacle climb gradient describes how much altitude must be gained for each nautical mile traveled over the ground. Pilots commonly see climb requirements stated in feet per nautical mile.

Gradient is not the same as feet per minute

A requirement of 300 feet per nautical mile does not directly tell you what vertical speed to hold. The required feet per minute depends on groundspeed. The faster you travel across the ground, the faster you must climb vertically to maintain the same gradient.

Convert gradient to vertical speed

A useful relationship is required FPM = climb gradient in ft/NM × groundspeed in knots ÷ 60. At 120 knots groundspeed, a 300 ft/NM gradient requires about 600 feet per minute.

Obstacle geometry versus aircraft capability

You can also estimate the geometric gradient needed to clear an obstacle from horizontal distance and height. That tells you what path is required. It does not prove the airplane can produce that climb under the actual weight, temperature, altitude, wind, and configuration.

Groundspeed matters

Headwind can reduce groundspeed and therefore reduce the FPM needed for a given gradient, while tailwind can increase the required FPM. Operational procedures may require more conservative assumptions, so use the published procedure and approved aircraft data.
