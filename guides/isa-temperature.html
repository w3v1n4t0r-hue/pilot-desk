ISA temperature by altitude

The International Standard Atmosphere gives pilots a common reference for temperature, pressure, and density. Near sea level, the standard temperature is 15°C. In the lower atmosphere used for most general aviation planning, temperature decreases at roughly 2°C per 1,000 feet.

Quick ISA temperature rule

A common training estimate is ISA temperature = 15°C minus 2°C for every 1,000 feet of altitude. At 5,000 feet, standard temperature is about 5°C. At 10,000 feet, it is about -5°C.

Why ISA matters

Comparing actual outside air temperature with ISA helps pilots understand whether the atmosphere is warmer or colder than standard. A warmer-than-standard day usually increases density altitude and can reduce aircraft performance.

ISA deviation

If the standard temperature at your altitude is 5°C and the actual temperature is 25°C, conditions are ISA +20. This kind of comparison appears often in performance discussions and turbine-aircraft operations.
