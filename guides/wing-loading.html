Aircraft wing loading explained

Wing loading is the aircraft's weight divided by wing area. It is commonly expressed in pounds per square foot. The number gives a simple way to compare how much weight each square foot of wing is supporting.

Wing loading formula

Wing loading = aircraft weight ÷ wing area. A 2,400-pound airplane with 160 square feet of wing area has a wing loading of 15 pounds per square foot.

What higher wing loading can mean

Aircraft with higher wing loading often have higher stall speeds and may feel less affected by small gusts, while aircraft with lower wing loading can operate at lower speeds and may respond more noticeably to turbulence. Real aircraft behavior depends on much more than one ratio.

Weight changes the answer

The same airplane has a different wing loading at different operating weights. If fuel burns off or payload changes, the weight-per-area value changes too.

Why pilots use it

Wing loading is useful for understanding design tradeoffs and comparing aircraft, but it is not a substitute for POH or AFM performance data.
