Aircraft ballast and center of gravity

Ballast is weight intentionally added to an aircraft to help place the center of gravity within an acceptable range. Where that weight is installed matters just as much as how much is added.

Why arm matters

Moment = weight × arm. A small amount of ballast placed far from the datum can create a larger moment change than the same weight placed closer to the datum.

Moving the CG

Adding weight aft of the current CG tends to move the CG aft. Adding weight forward tends to move it forward. The exact change depends on total aircraft weight, current moment, ballast weight, and ballast arm.

Ballast calculations

To estimate the ballast needed for a target CG, solve the weight-and-moment relationship using the current aircraft totals and the target arm. PilotDesk's calculator handles the arithmetic, but the allowable location and required installation method come from aircraft documentation.

Operational limits come first

Never add ballast simply because the math produces an answer. The airplane must remain within maximum weight, CG envelope, structural, baggage, and installation limits.
