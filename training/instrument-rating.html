Instrument ACS topics

The current FAA Instrument Rating – Airplane ACS listed by the FAA is FAA-S-ACS-8C. The strongest way to use it is to brief a complete IFR trip: weather, alternate thinking, fuel, route, clearances, departures, en route navigation, arrival, approach, missed approach, equipment failures, and what would make you stop the plan before launch.

Subjects to review

  • Instrument privileges, currency, inspections, equipment, and IFR flight-plan requirements.
  • Weather interpretation, icing and thunderstorm risk, ceilings and visibility, and deciding whether the approach is worth attempting.
  • Navigation systems, failure indications, RAIM/WAAS concepts where applicable, and how to keep a usable backup plan.
  • Departure, holding, arrival, approach, missed-approach, and circling concepts using current procedures.
  • Partial-panel and automation management without losing the basic attitude-instrument scan.

Tools for instrument practice

Top of Descent

Practice altitude-to-lose and gradient math.

Navigation Reference

Review course, heading, wind, DME, coordinates, and navlog relationships.

Oral-review prompts

Practice answering these aloud: When does an alternate become required, and what source are you using? What would make a legal alternate a bad choice? How do you know whether a GPS failure changes the approach you can fly? What is your plan if the weather drops while you are already in the hold? Where does the missed-approach procedure actually begin?

FAA references

Find the current standard for your certificate on the FAA Airman Certification Standards page. Use relevant chapters from the FAA handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and aircraft documents. For procedures, use the current chart and read its notes before briefing the approach.
