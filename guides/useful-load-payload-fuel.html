Aircraft Useful Load vs Payload vs Fuel

Useful load describes how much weight can be added to the aircraft above its defined empty weight before reaching a specified maximum weight. That available weight must be shared among people, baggage, fuel and other items.

Useful load

A common relationship is useful load = maximum allowable weight − empty weight, but the exact definitions depend on the aircraft's approved records and the weight limit being considered.

Payload

Payload usually refers to people, baggage or cargo carried for the mission. It is not always defined identically in every operating context, so use the definition required by the aircraft or operator.

Fuel is part of the loading tradeoff

Adding fuel uses some of the same available weight that could otherwise be used for passengers or baggage. “Full fuel” and “full seats” are not automatically compatible.

Worked concept

If an aircraft has 900 pounds of useful load and the people plus baggage total 650 pounds, only 250 pounds remain before reaching that weight limit. The fuel volume that fits within 250 pounds depends on fuel type and density.

CG matters too

Even when total weight is acceptable, passenger, baggage and fuel locations create moments that can move the CG outside the approved envelope. Weight and balance must be solved together.

Different maximum weights may apply

Aircraft may have different ramp, takeoff or landing weight limits. A loading condition that is acceptable at one phase may require planned fuel burn before another phase.
