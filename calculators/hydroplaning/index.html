How to use the Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed calculator

Formula and method

\n

8.6 × √(main-tire pressure in PSI)

\n

At 36 PSI, the FAA minimum dynamic hydroplaning estimate is 51.6 knots. The commonly rounded 9 × √PSI shortcut gives 54 knots at 36 PSI.

Dynamic hydroplaning onset is not a safe taxi, takeoff, or landing speed, and this calculation does not predict stopping distance. Tire pressure, runway surface and water depth, tire condition, braking, and aircraft factors affect hydroplaning. Use applicable aircraft guidance and runway condition information.

Source: FAA Airplane Flying Handbook, Chapter 9 (PDF). See also the PilotDesk hydroplaning guide.

Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed questions

What does the Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed calculator do?

It uses the entered aviation values to solve the dynamic hydroplaning speed relationship and displays the main outputs immediately for study, planning and cross-checking.

What inputs should I use?

For Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed, use values from the current source that applies to the problem. Keep the units and reference systems consistent with the performance relationship being solved.

Can I use the result as the only source for a flight?

No. Treat the Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed result as a calculation and training cross-check. Operational decisions still require the current aircraft data, weather, charts, procedures and regulations that apply.
