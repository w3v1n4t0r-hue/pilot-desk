How to build a VFR navlog

A VFR navigation log turns a route into manageable legs and records the math needed to estimate heading, groundspeed, time and fuel. The exact form varies, but the core logic is similar across paper and electronic navlogs.

Choose route and checkpoints

Start with current charts and appropriate checkpoints. Good checkpoints are identifiable, reasonably spaced and useful for confirming progress rather than merely filling rows on the log.

Measure course and distance

Determine the course for each leg and measure distance in nautical miles. Keep track of whether the course is true or magnetic before applying wind or variation.

Apply wind

Use forecast winds with true airspeed to solve the wind triangle. This gives wind correction angle and groundspeed. Apply magnetic variation as required to obtain the heading reference used for the flight.

Calculate leg time

Time = distance ÷ groundspeed. Convert decimal hours to minutes. Adding leg times produces an estimated time en route, but remember to account for climb, descent and other phases when appropriate.

Calculate fuel

Fuel for each segment depends on planned fuel flow and time. A complete fuel plan also accounts for start, taxi, climb, reserve and any operator- or aircraft-specific planning requirements.

Record frequencies and notes

A navlog may include frequencies, airport information, altitudes or other planning notes. Use current official data rather than copying old values from a previous flight.

Update in flight

Actual groundspeed and checkpoint timing may differ from the plan. Comparing actual versus planned values helps refine ETA and fuel predictions.
