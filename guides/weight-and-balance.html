Aircraft weight and balance basics

Weight and balance is mostly a running total of weights and moments. The arithmetic is simple; the important part is using the correct aircraft-specific loading data and comparing the final result with the approved envelope.

Weight, arm and moment

Moment = Weight × Arm

The arm is the distance from the aircraft datum to the item. Add the moments for the empty airplane, occupants, fuel, baggage and any other loading stations.

Find the center of gravity

CG = Total Moment ÷ Total Weight

The result is only useful when compared with the approved CG envelope for the airplane at that weight.

Fuel changes the loading

Fuel burn changes both weight and moment. Depending on tank location, the CG can move as fuel is consumed. A single takeoff calculation does not automatically prove the airplane remains within limits through landing.

Profiles save time, not verification

PilotDesk aircraft profiles can remember common station arms and empty-weight data on your device. Treat saved values as a convenience. Verify them against the current aircraft records and POH/AFM before relying on them.

Worked station example

A 180-pound passenger sitting at an 85-inch arm contributes 15,300 lb-in of moment. Do that for the empty aircraft, fuel, passengers, and baggage, then add the weights and moments. Total moment divided by total weight gives the loaded center of gravity. The answer still has to be checked against the approved envelope for that aircraft and loading condition.

Why both weight and CG matter

Being under maximum gross weight does not automatically mean the loading is acceptable. A CG that is too far forward or aft can affect control forces, stability, stall behavior, rotation, flare, and recovery characteristics. Use the current aircraft weight-and-balance record and POH or AFM limits rather than a generic preset.
