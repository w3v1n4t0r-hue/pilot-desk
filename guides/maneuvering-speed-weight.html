Maneuvering speed at a lower weight

Maneuvering speed, usually written Va, is associated with the relationship between aerodynamic stall and structural load. A key training point is that Va is not one fixed number for every loading condition: the appropriate value decreases as aircraft weight decreases.

Why lower weight means lower Va

At a lighter weight, the wing needs less lift to support the airplane. That means the aircraft can reach its critical angle of attack at a lower speed for the same load factor. The resulting maneuvering-speed relationship follows the square root of the weight ratio.

Common estimate

A frequently used estimate is Va at current weight = published Va at reference weight × square root of current weight ÷ reference weight. Both weights must use the same units.

Worked example

If published Va is 113 knots at 2,550 pounds and current weight is 2,100 pounds, the ratio is 2,100 ÷ 2,550. Taking the square root and multiplying by 113 gives an estimate near 103 knots.

What Va does not mean

Va is not a guarantee that any control input is harmless. Abrupt full control inputs in more than one axis, repeated inputs, turbulence and aircraft-specific limitations can still create unsafe loads. The approved handbook definition and limitations control.

Published tables are better

If the POH/AFM provides maneuvering speeds at several weights, use those approved values rather than a generic estimate. The square-root relationship is most useful for training or interpolation when approved guidance permits it.

Weight changes during flight

As fuel burns, aircraft weight decreases. That means a maneuvering-speed value appropriate at takeoff may be higher than the value appropriate later in flight.
