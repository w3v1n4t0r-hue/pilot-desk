What pressure altitude means

Pressure altitude is the altitude indicated when the altimeter is set to standard pressure, 29.92 inHg. It expresses the ambient pressure on a standard-atmosphere altitude scale and is a common input to approved aircraft performance charts. It is not the airplane's true height above terrain.

Pressure altitude ≈ field elevation + (29.92 − altimeter setting) × 1,000

Worked example

For a hypothetical field elevation of 5,883 ft and altimeter setting 29.42 inHg: correction = (29.92 − 29.42) × 1,000 = +500 ft; estimated pressure altitude = 5,883 + 500 = 6,383 ft. A setting below 29.92 raises pressure altitude above field elevation; a setting above 29.92 lowers it. The 1,000-ft-per-inch conversion is a training approximation.

Use the current setting and the method the aircraft's performance chart requires. Next, the Density Altitude Calculator combines pressure altitude and outside air temperature to estimate an atmospheric density equivalent; density altitude alone does not supply takeoff or climb performance.

See the pressure altitude guide, the FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, and PilotDesk Sources & Methods.

Pressure Altitude questions

What is pressure altitude used for?

Pressure altitude is a standard reference used in aircraft performance calculations, density-altitude work and many POH or AFM charts.

Why is 29.92 inHg important?

29.92 inHg is standard sea-level pressure in the U.S. altimeter system and is the reference setting used to define pressure altitude.

Is the 1,000-feet-per-inch formula exact?

No. It is a common training approximation. Use the method specified by the aircraft or chart when precision matters.
