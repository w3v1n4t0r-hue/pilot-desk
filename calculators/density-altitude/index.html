How to use this calculator

Use current pressure, temperature, and weather data. If the number affects a flight, check it against the aircraft performance information and current weather products you are using.

Enter Pressure altitude, OAT. The calculator returns Density altitude, ISA temperature, Temperature deviation. Check the inputs before relying on the result.

Quick check

The sample values are: Pressure altitude 5000 ft; OAT 30 °C. Run the calculation, change one input, and run it again to see how the result changes. For a real flight, replace the sample values with current information and check the result against the applicable POH/AFM, chart, weather product, or navigation source.

Show formula and assumptions

PilotDesk derives air density from pressure altitude and temperature, then converts that density back to an ISA-equivalent altitude.

Units are shown beside each field and result. Aircraft-specific corrections, interpolation rules, reserves, runway factors, and operating limits are not included unless the page says they are.

Common questions

Why does density altitude matter?

Higher density altitude reduces air density and can reduce aircraft, engine, and propeller performance. Actual takeoff, climb, and landing performance must be determined from current approved aircraft performance data.

Is density altitude the same as pressure altitude?

No. Pressure altitude corrects altitude for nonstandard pressure. Density altitude further accounts for air density, including temperature effects.

Can I use this instead of the POH performance charts?

No. This calculator is a study and cross-check tool. Use the aircraft POH or AFM performance procedure for operational planning.
