Aircraft endurance and range

Endurance describes how long an aircraft can remain airborne with a given amount of usable fuel and fuel flow. Range describes how far it can travel during that time. They are related, but wind affects range much more directly than endurance.

Basic endurance formula

Estimated endurance = usable fuel available ÷ fuel flow. If 36 gallons are available and the aircraft burns 9 gallons per hour, the simple arithmetic gives 4 hours before accounting for reserve, unusable fuel, start/taxi fuel or changes in power setting.

Reserve changes usable planning time

For practical planning, subtract the fuel that must remain as reserve and any other planned fuel allowances before computing trip endurance. Regulatory fuel requirements depend on the operation and conditions, so the arithmetic is not a substitute for the applicable rule.

Range uses groundspeed

Estimated range = usable planning endurance × groundspeed. If 3 hours of planning endurance remain after reserve and groundspeed is 110 knots, the simple range estimate is 330 NM.

Headwinds and tailwinds

A headwind reduces groundspeed and therefore reduces distance covered for the same fuel burn. A tailwind does the opposite. That is why range estimates should be updated with realistic winds rather than based only on true airspeed.

Fuel flow is not always constant

Climb, cruise, descent and mixture settings can have different fuel flows. A more realistic navlog breaks the flight into phases or uses approved performance data instead of assuming one fuel flow for the entire trip.

Use conservative inputs

Planning with optimistic groundspeed or unusually low fuel flow can make a range number look better than reality. Cross-check the result against the aircraft POH/AFM and leave appropriate margins.
