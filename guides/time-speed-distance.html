Time, speed and distance formula for pilots

Time-speed-distance math is one of the most useful pieces of pilot arithmetic because it appears in navlogs, fuel planning, ETA checks, diversions and E6B work. The three quantities are linked by one simple relationship: distance equals speed multiplied by time.

The three formulas

Distance = speed × time. Time = distance ÷ speed. Speed = distance ÷ time. In aviation, speed is often in knots and distance is in nautical miles, so time must be expressed in hours for the units to work directly.

Convert minutes to hours

If a leg takes 24 minutes, divide by 60 before multiplying by knots. Twenty-four minutes is 0.4 hour. At 120 knots, 120 × 0.4 gives 48 nautical miles. For a quick cockpit shortcut, nautical miles per minute equals groundspeed divided by 60.

Worked flight-planning example

Suppose the planned groundspeed is 105 knots and the leg is 52.5 NM. Time is 52.5 ÷ 105 = 0.5 hour, or 30 minutes. If actual groundspeed later falls to 90 knots, the same leg would take 35 minutes. That difference matters when you update ETA and fuel remaining.

Mental-math checkpoints

At 60 knots you travel about 1 NM per minute. At 90 knots it is 1.5 NM per minute, at 120 knots it is 2 NM per minute, and at 180 knots it is 3 NM per minute. These checkpoints make it easier to catch a calculator entry that is obviously wrong.

Common mistakes

Do not mix statute miles with knots, do not multiply by minutes without converting to hours, and use groundspeed rather than true airspeed when calculating time over the ground. Wind changes groundspeed, so planned and actual leg times can differ even when the airplane's airspeed is unchanged.
