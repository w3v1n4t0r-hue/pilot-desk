How to Calculate Density Altitude

Density altitude is pressure altitude corrected for nonstandard air density. Pilots use it because an airplane can perform as though it were operating at a much higher altitude than the airport elevation when the air is hot and thin.

Density altitude formula

A common training approximation is:

Density altitude ≈ Pressure altitude + 120 × (OAT − ISA temperature)

Use pressure altitude in feet and temperatures in degrees Celsius. The 120 feet per degree Celsius relationship is a cockpit and classroom approximation. PilotDesk's calculator instead derives dry-air density from a standard-atmosphere model and converts that density to an ISA-equivalent altitude, so its answer may differ somewhat from the shortcut.

Step 1: calculate pressure altitude

Density altitude starts with pressure altitude, not simply field elevation. A widely used quick estimate is:

Pressure altitude ≈ Field elevation + (29.92 − altimeter setting) × 1,000

For example, an airport at 5,000 feet MSL with an altimeter setting of 30.12 inHg has an approximate pressure altitude of 4,800 feet: 5,000 + (29.92 − 30.12) × 1,000.

Step 2: find ISA temperature

In the lower standard atmosphere, temperature decreases by about 2°C per 1,000 feet. A common estimate is:

ISA temperature ≈ 15°C − 2°C per 1,000 ft

At roughly 4,800 feet pressure altitude, ISA temperature is near 5°C. If the actual outside-air temperature is 30°C, the air is about 25°C warmer than standard.

Step 3: apply the temperature correction

Using the quick formula, 4,800 + 120 × (30 − 5) gives approximately 7,800 feet density altitude. The airport is physically near 5,000 feet, but the airplane is operating in air whose density resembles standard air much higher in the atmosphere.

Pressure altitude vs. density altitude

Pressure altitude is primarily a pressure reference. Density altitude describes air density in terms of an equivalent standard-atmosphere altitude. Temperature can make the two numbers very different. On a cold day, density altitude can be below pressure altitude. On a hot day, especially at a high-elevation airport, density altitude can be thousands of feet above it.

What raises density altitude?

Three major conditions push density altitude higher: high field elevation, low atmospheric pressure, and high temperature. Humidity also lowers air density, though simple 120-feet-per-degree training calculations usually do not include a humidity correction. For aircraft performance, use the exact method and data specified by the aircraft manufacturer.

Why high density altitude hurts aircraft performance

Lower air density can reduce wing, propeller, and normally aspirated engine performance. The airplane may need more runway to accelerate, produce less climb performance, and develop less available engine power. The exact effect is aircraft-specific, which is why calculating density altitude is only the beginning of the performance problem.

After finding density altitude, move to the current POH or AFM performance charts for the actual weight, runway, wind, temperature, configuration, and conditions. A density-altitude number by itself is not a takeoff-distance calculation.

Common density altitude mistakes

Avoid using field elevation when the problem requires pressure altitude. Keep Celsius and Fahrenheit straight. Use current temperature and pressure rather than stale weather. Most importantly, do not turn a density-altitude value into a performance guarantee. A heavy airplane on a short runway can have a very different risk picture than a light airplane at the same density altitude.

Density altitude questions

What is the quickest way to estimate density altitude?

Find pressure altitude, estimate ISA temperature at that altitude, then use DA ≈ PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA temperature).

Can density altitude be lower than field elevation?

Yes. Cold, dense air can produce a density altitude below the physical field elevation.

Does humidity increase density altitude?

Humidity reduces air density and can worsen performance. Many simple classroom density-altitude formulas do not explicitly include humidity, so follow the aircraft performance method that applies.

Is density altitude the same as takeoff performance?

No. It is one atmospheric input. Use the aircraft's approved performance charts or method to determine takeoff, climb, and landing performance.
