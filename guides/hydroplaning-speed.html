Dynamic hydroplaning speed formula

Dynamic hydroplaning can occur when a tire rides on a film of water instead of maintaining normal contact with the runway. Pilot training often uses a tire-pressure rule of thumb to estimate the speed where dynamic hydroplaning can begin.

The common rule

The familiar estimate is 9 × square root of tire pressure in psi. The result is in knots. If main-tire pressure is 36 psi, the square root is 6, and the estimated dynamic hydroplaning speed is about 54 knots.

What the number means

The calculation is not a precise threshold where hydroplaning suddenly starts or stops. Runway water depth, tire condition, tread, braking, runway texture and aircraft characteristics all matter. The formula is best treated as a training estimate that illustrates why higher tire pressure corresponds to a higher characteristic speed.

Landing risk

Wet-runway stopping distance can increase even without full dynamic hydroplaning. Touchdown speed, crosswind, braking action, runway length and contamination should all be considered using current approved guidance and airport information.

Takeoff considerations

A contaminated runway can affect both acceleration and rejected-takeoff performance. Do not use the hydroplaning formula as a substitute for aircraft performance data or runway-condition guidance.

Reasonableness check

For light-aircraft tire pressures in the 25 to 50 psi range, the simple estimate will usually land in roughly the mid-40s to mid-60s knots. A result far outside that range is a good reason to recheck the input and units.
