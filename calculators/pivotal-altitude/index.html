Pivotal altitude formula

Pivotal altitude (ft AGL) ≈ Groundspeed² ÷ 11.3

The 11.3 divisor applies when groundspeed is in knots and the answer is in feet AGL. Do not plug indicated airspeed into this relationship: pivotal altitude depends on movement over the ground.

Why wind changes the result

Groundspeed normally changes as the airplane moves around an eights-on-pylons maneuver. A higher groundspeed produces a higher pivotal altitude; a lower groundspeed produces a lower one. That is why the pivotal relationship is not one fixed altitude for the entire maneuver in wind.

100-knot example

At 100 knots, 100² ÷ 11.3 is about 885 feet AGL. At 120 knots, the answer rises to about 1,274 feet AGL. Because speed is squared, the altitude changes faster than groundspeed itself.

Pivotal altitude questions

What is pivotal altitude at 100 knots?

Approximately 885 feet AGL using the common knots formula.

What is pivotal altitude at 120 knots?

Approximately 1,274 feet AGL.

Is pivotal altitude AGL or MSL?

The formula produces a height above the ground reference. Add local ground elevation only when you need an MSL comparison.

Does bank angle appear in the formula?

No. The common pivotal-altitude relationship is driven by groundspeed. Bank is used to maintain the maneuver geometry and ground track.
