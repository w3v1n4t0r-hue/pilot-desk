Interpolating POH performance charts

Aircraft performance charts often publish values at selected temperatures, altitudes, weights or wind conditions rather than every possible point. Interpolation estimates a value between published points when the chart or procedure allows it.

Read the entire chart first

Before doing any math, identify the chart title, units, notes, conditions and limitations. Performance charts may assume a specific weight, configuration, runway surface, technique or power setting.

Linear interpolation idea

If the desired condition lies halfway between two published inputs, a simple linear interpolation places the output halfway between their values. If the input is one quarter of the way across the interval, use one quarter of the output difference.

Worked example

If a chart gives 1,000 feet at one condition and 1,200 feet at the next, a halfway condition would be estimated at 1,100 feet only when linear interpolation is appropriate for that chart.

Do not extrapolate casually

Interpolation estimates between known points. Extrapolation goes beyond published data and can be unreliable or prohibited. If the operating condition is outside the chart, the chart may not support the operation.

Multiple variables

Many performance problems require repeated interpolation for pressure altitude, temperature, weight or wind. Work one variable at a time and keep the units visible so you can audit the process.

Chart reading errors can dominate the math

A perfectly calculated interpolation is useless if the wrong line, axis or condition was selected. Cross-check the result against nearby chart values and the expected trend.
