Your job is to make the learner think, not just hear you talk

The FAA lists Flight Instructor for Airplane Category ACS FAA-S-ACS-25. It begins with Fundamentals of Instructing and then asks the applicant to teach and demonstrate aeronautical knowledge, risk management, and flight skills. A CFI lesson that is technically correct but confusing is still a weak lesson.

Build lessons around a simple structure

  • State the objective in plain language: what should the learner be able to explain or do at the end?
  • Find out what the learner already understands before filling ten minutes with material they know.
  • Use a short explanation, a real example, and a chance for the learner to make a decision.
  • Ask questions that reveal understanding instead of questions that can be answered with one memorized word.
  • Give specific feedback and connect the correction to the standard or safety reason.

Teaching prompts

How would you explain adverse yaw to someone who has never heard the term? What question would reveal whether a student really understands density altitude? How would you correct a student who can recite the checklist but misses the reason for an item? What risk-management habit are you trying to build during this lesson, not just what maneuver are you trying to finish?

Use these sources first

The practical test is built from the FAA standard in effect on the day of the test. Start with the FAA Airman Certification Standards page, then use the current FAA aviation handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and your aircraft documents. The FAA Aviation Instructor’s Handbook is also listed on the FAA aviation-handbooks page and should be a primary source for Fundamentals of Instructing study.
