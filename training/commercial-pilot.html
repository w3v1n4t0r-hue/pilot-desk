Commercial is more than tighter maneuver tolerances

The FAA lists Commercial Pilot for Airplane Category ACS FAA-S-ACS-7B. It expects you to bring private-pilot knowledge forward, apply stronger judgment, understand commercial privileges and limitations, and demonstrate the aircraft with much more deliberate energy management.

Study areas worth connecting

  • Commercial privileges and limitations, common-versus-private carriage concepts, and when operating rules beyond part 61 matter.
  • Performance planning using the actual airplane, loading, runway, weather, and obstacle picture.
  • Systems and abnormal procedures at a level where you can explain what is happening, not just recite a switch position.
  • Energy management in chandelles, lazy eights, steep turns, eights on pylons, power-off accuracy work, and other tasks that apply to your test.
  • Risk management around passengers, schedule pressure, weather, maintenance, and a more professional operating mindset.

Oral-review prompts

Could you take money for this flight, and under what authority? Why does pivotal altitude move when groundspeed changes? What happens to stall speed as load factor rises? What aircraft-specific numbers would you verify before attempting a short-field departure on a hot day? If the engine quits during a power-off accuracy approach, what part of the maneuver suddenly stops being a checkride exercise?

Use these sources first

The practical test is built from the FAA standard in effect on the day of the test. Start with the FAA Airman Certification Standards page, then use the current FAA aviation handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and your aircraft documents.
