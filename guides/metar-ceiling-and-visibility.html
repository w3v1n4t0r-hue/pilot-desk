METAR ceiling and visibility

Ceiling and visibility are two of the quickest ways to understand the basic operating environment reported by a METAR. They also feed the common color-coded flight categories shown by aviation weather sites.

Visibility

U.S. METARs normally report prevailing visibility in statute miles. A value such as 10SM means ten statute miles, while fractional values indicate more restricted visibility.

Sky cover

Common sky-cover codes include FEW, SCT, BKN and OVC for few, scattered, broken and overcast. The number that follows is the cloud-base height in hundreds of feet above ground level.

What counts as a ceiling

The ceiling is the lowest layer reported as broken or overcast, or the vertical visibility into an obscuration. Few and scattered layers do not by themselves establish a ceiling.

Example

If a METAR shows SCT020 BKN035 OVC060, the lowest ceiling is the broken layer at 3,500 feet AGL. The scattered layer at 2,000 feet is still operationally relevant but is not the reported ceiling.

Vertical visibility

When the sky is obscured, a VV group may report how far upward an observer or sensor can see. Vertical visibility can therefore serve as the ceiling in obscured conditions.

Flight category

The Aviation Weather Center combines ceiling and visibility to classify VFR, MVFR, IFR or LIFR. The worse of the ceiling or visibility conditions can determine the displayed category.

Do not stop at the category color

Cloud layers, weather phenomena, trends, terrain, approach minimums and pilot or aircraft limitations can all matter beyond the simple ceiling/visibility classification.
