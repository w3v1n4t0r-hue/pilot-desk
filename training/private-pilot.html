Build the private-pilot picture, not a pile of trivia

The Private Pilot for Airplane Category ACS (FAA-S-ACS-6C as listed by the FAA in September 2026) mixes knowledge, risk management, and flying skill. A useful study session should do the same. Pick a real route and airplane. Explain whether the flight is legal, whether it is smart, what the weather is doing, what the airplane can actually do, and what you would change if one part of the plan got worse.

Good places to spend your time

  • Airworthiness, required documents, inspections, equipment, and pilot privileges and limitations.
  • Weather products and the difference between reading a report and making a decision from it.
  • Cross-country planning: course, wind correction, groundspeed, time, fuel, alternates, airspace, and runway considerations.
  • Weight and balance and performance using the actual aircraft data, not generic numbers.
  • Aerodynamics, stalls, load factor, maneuvering speed, and the reasons behind the maneuver standards.
  • Emergency planning, diversion thinking, systems knowledge, and risk management.

Oral-review prompts

These are study prompts, not FAA test questions: What makes the airplane airworthy today? What weather change would make you delay this trip? Which performance number in the POH worries you most on a hot day? If the alternator fails in cruise, what equipment do you still have and how long can you reasonably expect it to last? If your planned runway gets a stronger quartering tailwind, what changes?

Use these sources first

The practical test is built from the FAA standard in effect on the day of the test. Start with the FAA Airman Certification Standards page, then use the current FAA aviation handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and your aircraft documents.
