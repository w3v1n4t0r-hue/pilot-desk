Crosswind Component Chart

You do not need to memorize a giant chart. Learn a few angle factors, know what the calculator is doing, and slow down when the result is anywhere near an aircraft, operator, or personal limit.

The chart is just the sine and cosine relationship

If the wind is 20 knots and 30° off the runway, the crosswind component is 20 × sin(30°), or 10 knots. The headwind component is 20 × cos(30°), about 17 knots. The FAA Pilot’s Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge includes a crosswind/headwind component chart; the table below is a quick mental-math companion, not a replacement for current aircraft or operator limits.

A faster cockpit estimate

At 30°, crosswind is about one-half the wind. At 45°, it is about seven-tenths. At 60°, it is about 0.87. At 90°, all of the wind is crosswind. Those are estimates; if the number is close to a limit, calculate it carefully and use the exact wind and runway information.
