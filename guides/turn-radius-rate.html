Turn radius and rate of turn

Two ideas describe a coordinated level turn: how quickly the aircraft changes heading and how large the circle is. Airspeed and bank angle affect both, which is why a faster airplane needs either more bank or more space to achieve the same turning performance.

What increases turn rate?

At a given airspeed, increasing bank angle increases rate of turn and decreases turn radius. At a given bank angle, increasing true airspeed decreases rate of turn and increases turn radius.

Why speed has such a strong effect

Turn radius grows with the square of speed. That means a relatively small increase in speed can noticeably enlarge the path over the ground. This is important when thinking about pattern spacing, maneuvering and obstacle clearance.

Standard-rate context

A standard-rate turn changes heading at about 3 degrees per second, completing 360 degrees in roughly two minutes. Because the required bank increases with true airspeed, pilots often use quick rules of thumb and then verify the actual turn with the turn coordinator or other approved instrumentation.

Worked example

An aircraft at 100 knots TAS in a 20-degree bank will have a smaller radius and higher turn rate than the same aircraft at 140 knots with the same bank. Increasing the bank at 140 knots can recover turn rate, but it also increases load factor.

Do not separate turn math from load factor

Steeper coordinated level turns require more lift and increase load factor. That raises stall speed, so a tighter turn is not free performance. Use the aircraft's limitations and maneuvering guidance.
