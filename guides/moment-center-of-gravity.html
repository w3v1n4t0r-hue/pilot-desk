Aircraft moment and center of gravity

Weight-and-balance math is built around three basic values: weight, arm, and moment. Understanding their relationship makes it easier to catch bad entries before they become a loading problem.

Weight times arm equals moment

Moment = weight × arm. If a 170-pound occupant sits at a 37-inch station, that loading item creates 6,290 pound-inches of moment.

Total CG formula

Add all aircraft weights to get total weight. Add all moments to get total moment. Then divide total moment by total weight to calculate the loaded center-of-gravity arm.

Why moment can be scaled

Some aircraft documents divide moments by 100 or 1,000 to keep chart numbers smaller. Keep the scaling consistent throughout the calculation and follow the aircraft's approved loading form.

Reasonableness checks

If a small loading change moves the calculated CG dramatically, recheck units, station arms, signs, and any moment index scaling. A calculator can remove arithmetic mistakes but cannot know whether the source numbers were entered correctly.
