Pressure Altitude

Pressure altitude is the altitude the airplane would indicate with the altimeter set to 29.92 inHg. Pilots use it as a common atmospheric reference for performance calculations.

Quick calculation

Pressure altitude ≈ field elevation + (29.92 − altimeter setting) × 1,000

If the altimeter setting is below 29.92, pressure altitude is higher than field elevation. If the setting is above 29.92, pressure altitude is lower.

Example

At a 1,000 ft field with an altimeter setting of 29.42, the pressure correction is about +500 ft, giving a pressure altitude of about 1,500 ft.

Why it matters

Pressure altitude is one input in density-altitude and aircraft performance work. For an actual flight, use the current altimeter setting and the aircraft manufacturer's approved performance information rather than treating this shortcut as aircraft performance data.
