PILOTDESK LEARN · ORAL EXAM

Study the way the checkride is actually conducted.

The oral is not a vocabulary contest. An examiner gives you a certificate, an airplane, a route, and a problem. You need to explain the rule, make a sound decision, and know where to look when the detail matters.

Use the current source. This guide is an original study aid. The current ACS, regulations, AIM, handbooks, aircraft documents, and your instructor control.

THE STUDY METHOD

Every topic has four passes.

01 · KNOW

State the rule or concept in plain language. Know the limits, exceptions, and definitions that change the answer.

02 · SAY

Practice an answer you could give across a table. Lead with the decision, then explain the reason.

03 · APPLY

Put the topic into a real airplane and route. Weather, loading, equipment, and passengers should change your answer.

04 · VERIFY

Open the ACS task, regulation, AIM paragraph, handbook chapter, or aircraft document that controls the detail.

COMMON ORAL LANES

Where examiners spend time.

Airworthiness and documents

Be ready to identify the aircraft and pilot documents, inspections, required equipment, inoperative-equipment decisions, and the difference between legal dispatch and a good decision.

Verify in 14 CFR Part 91 →
Weather and go / no-go judgment

Decode the report, describe the trend, find the hazards, and say what would make you delay, divert, or continue. “The METAR is VFR” is not a complete weather briefing.

Use the FAA handbooks →
Performance and loading

Use the aircraft’s approved data. Explain density altitude, runway and obstacle margins, center of gravity, fuel decisions, and what changes when the numbers move.

Practice weight and balance →
Regulations and privileges

Know which rule answers the question. Separate certificate privileges, ratings, endorsements, currency, limitations, and operating rules.

Verify in 14 CFR Part 61 →
Navigation, procedures, and failures

Talk through the route, clearance, procedure, missed approach, lost communications or equipment failure, and the backup plan without relying on a moving map to think for you.
