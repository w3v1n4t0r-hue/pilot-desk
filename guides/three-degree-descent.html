3 Degree Descent Rate Formula and Rule

A three-degree descent path is common in approach and descent planning. The faster the airplane moves over the ground, the faster it must descend in feet per minute to remain on the same geometric path.

Exact 3 degree descent rate formula

The geometric relationship is:

Vertical speed = Groundspeed × 6,076.12 ÷ 60 × tan(3°)

With groundspeed in knots, the result is feet per minute. The constants simplify to approximately:

Vertical speed ≈ Groundspeed × 5.3

At 120 knots groundspeed, an exact three-degree path is about 637 fpm. At 150 knots it is about 796 fpm.

The groundspeed × 5 rule

The familiar cockpit shortcut is:

Vertical speed ≈ Groundspeed × 5

At 120 knots, that gives 600 fpm instead of the exact geometric value near 637 fpm. At 150 knots, the shortcut gives 750 fpm instead of about 796 fpm. The shortcut is intentionally easy to calculate mentally and is useful as a starting estimate.

Use groundspeed, not airspeed

The vertical speed required for a geometric path depends on groundspeed. A headwind lowers groundspeed and therefore lowers the required descent rate. A tailwind raises groundspeed and raises the required rate. If groundspeed changes significantly during an approach or descent, the target vertical speed changes with it.

The 3 to 1 descent rule

A second common planning shortcut estimates the horizontal distance needed to lose altitude:

Descent distance ≈ 3 NM per 1,000 ft to lose

If you need to lose 6,000 feet, a rough three-degree descent begins about 18 NM before the target point. If you need to lose 3,000 feet, think roughly 9 NM. This is a planning estimate, not authorization to descend and not a substitute for published restrictions.

Why the 3 to 1 rule works

A true three-degree geometric path covers a little more than 3 NM horizontally for each 1,000 feet of vertical change. The 3:1 shortcut is close enough to make quick descent planning easy and pairs naturally with the groundspeed × 5 vertical-speed estimate.

Worked descent example

Suppose you are 12,000 feet and want to be at 3,000 feet at a fix. You need to lose 9,000 feet. The 3:1 rule suggests starting about 27 NM before the fix. If groundspeed is 140 knots, the quick rate is roughly 700 fpm; the exact 3° geometric rate is about 743 fpm.

This does not account for level segments, speed changes, ATC restrictions, configuration changes, or a required deceleration before the fix. Those items may require beginning the descent earlier.

3 degree path vs. published procedure

A calculated three-degree path is only geometry. On an actual instrument approach, use the published procedure, vertical guidance, altitude restrictions, and applicable stabilized-approach criteria. A calculator should not be used to replace a glideslope, glidepath, VDA, or charted procedure requirement.

3 degree descent questions

What vertical speed should I use at 100 knots groundspeed?

About 531 fpm for exact 3° geometry, or 500 fpm using the common groundspeed × 5 shortcut.

What about 120 knots?

About 637 fpm exact, with a quick-rule estimate of 600 fpm.

Does a headwind change the descent rate?

Yes. A headwind reduces groundspeed, so a lower feet-per-minute descent rate is needed to remain on the same geometric path.

Is the 3:1 rule exact?

No. It is a cockpit planning shortcut. Use published procedures and guidance when they control the descent.
