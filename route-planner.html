    Offline flight packs

    Build a route, zoom to the chart area you need, then download it. The pack includes this planner, resolved route, aircraft planning inputs, selected FAA procedure PDFs and chart tiles within the visible bounds. Maximum 800 tiles / 100 MB. An incomplete download is discarded. Browser storage can be evicted; reopen the pack offline before departure to verify it. Navigation expires with its FAA edition; chart imagery expires no later than seven days after capture and requires printed edition verification.

    Download while connected. Weather and briefing APIs are never stored as current offline data.

    FAA routing & procedure segments

    Enter FIX V123 FIX to expand an airway, or DCT between fixes. Navigation uses the FAA CIFP edition covering your UTC departure. Airway expansion shows published fixes; verify chart altitude, restrictions, NOTAMs and clearance.

    Build a route to check the navigation edition.

    Segments are separate runway, common or enroute branches, not an assembled clearance. Automatic insertion supports IF/TF/DF fixes only, requires a matching join, and excludes missed approaches. Published constraints remain chart requirements.

    Climb, cruise & descent

    Enter average performance from your aircraft documentation for the planned weight, altitude and temperature. These rates stay constant; this is not a POH chart solver. Route and leg TAS/burn inputs apply to cruise. Each leg wind applies to all three phases.

    Aircraft POH performance table

    Enter verified aircraft data as JSON rows. Columns: pressureAltitude (ft), oat (°C), weight (lb), cruiseTas, cruiseBurn, climbTas, climbBurn, climbRate, descentTas, descentBurn, descentRate. Speeds are TAS kt, burns US gal/hr and rates ft/min. Use one configuration. Linear interpolation requires every surrounding table corner; no extrapolation. Climb/descent use 500 ft midpoint bands. Enter the pressure altitude offset and atmospheric temperature lapse explicitly; weight remains constant. Leg cruise overrides still take precedence.

    Forecast winds aloft

    AWC/NWS forecast for the contiguous United States. Uses the station nearest each leg midpoint (within 200 NM) and interpolates wind vectors between altitude levels. Requires cruise altitude and UTC departure. Light/variable winds are treated as calm. No terrain or horizontal weather interpolation.

    Before you save the flight

    • Confirm each waypoint and the chart edition.
    • Use forecast enroute winds for the navlog; the METAR shown here is airport context only.
    • Check terrain, airspace, TFRs, NOTAMs, and procedures from current sources.
    • Compare the time and fuel result with the aircraft data and fuel requirement that actually apply to the flight.

    How the navlog is calculated

    Leg distance and initial true course use great-circle geometry. Wind correction solves the wind triangle from true course, TAS, wind direction, and wind speed. Magnetic heading applies the user-entered variation. ETE is leg distance divided by groundspeed, and fuel is time multiplied by the entered hourly burn.

    Surface METAR wind is shown for airport context only. It is not used for the enroute wind calculation. The chart background comes from FAA AIS public raster tile services, with selectable VFR and IFR layers; coverage depends on the published chart products and requires connectivity. Airport, NAVAID, and fix search adds available points to a route, and supported airport endpoints can show current METAR context. The navlog calculates great-circle leg distance/course, wind-corrected heading and groundspeed from the wind you enter, ETE, and estimated fuel. Saved routes can be reopened where available. Terminal-procedure links are current FAA d-TPP documents and remain FAA-hosted PDFs. The planner does not replace a complete official briefing or independently validate airspace, NOTAMs, route legality, or aircraft performance.
