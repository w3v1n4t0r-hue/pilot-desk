INSTRUMENT

Instrument Rating Study Hub

Instrument study gets easier when every topic belongs to the same IFR flight. Build one route and keep asking what you would do if the weather, equipment, or clearance changed.

Think in terms of an IFR flight from start to finish

The current FAA Instrument Rating – Airplane ACS listed by the FAA is FAA-S-ACS-8C. The strongest way to use it is to brief a complete IFR trip: weather, alternate thinking, fuel, route, clearances, departures, en route navigation, arrival, approach, missed approach, equipment failures, and what would make you stop the plan before launch.

Core study areas

  • Instrument privileges, currency, inspections, equipment, and IFR flight-plan requirements.
  • Weather interpretation, icing and thunderstorm risk, ceilings and visibility, and deciding whether the approach is worth attempting.
  • Navigation systems, failure indications, RAIM/WAAS concepts where applicable, and how to keep a usable backup plan.
  • Departure, holding, arrival, approach, missed-approach, and circling concepts using current procedures.
  • Partial-panel and automation management without losing the basic attitude-instrument scan.

Oral-review prompts

Talk through these out loud: When does an alternate become required, and what source are you using? What would make a legal alternate a bad choice? How do you know whether a GPS failure changes the approach you can fly? What is your plan if the weather drops while you are already in the hold? Where does the missed-approach procedure actually begin?

Use these sources first

The practical test is built from the FAA standard in effect on the day of the test. Start with the FAA Airman Certification Standards page, then use the current FAA aviation handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and your aircraft documents. For procedures, use the current chart and applicable notes; PilotDesk does not reproduce a legal briefing package.
