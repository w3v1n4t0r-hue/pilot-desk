Standard-rate turn: rate, bank angle, and formula

A standard-rate turn changes heading at approximately 3° per second. At that rate, 360° takes two minutes. The bank angle needed for a chosen turn rate rises with true airspeed, so there is no single bank angle that produces a standard-rate turn at every speed.

Turn-rate relationship

For a coordinated, level turn at ordinary subsonic airplane speeds, the relationship is approximately:

Rate of turn (degrees/second) = 1,091 × tan(bank angle) ÷ TAS (knots)

Rearranged for bank angle, bank = arctan(rate × TAS ÷ 1,091). This idealized geometry explains the relationship; it does not replace the turn-rate indication, the aircraft's limitations, or instruction.

Worked example at 120 kt TAS

For a target rate of 3° per second at 120 kt TAS, bank = arctan(3 × 120 ÷ 1,091), which is about 18.2°. The common 15%-of-TAS shortcut gives 18°. That mental estimate is close in this case, but the geometric result changes with airspeed.

At 180 kt TAS, the same formula gives about 26.3° of bank for 3° per second. This illustrates why a memorized 15° or 20° bank value cannot guarantee a standard-rate turn across speeds.

Use the indication and the procedure

The turn coordinator or approved display indicates the actual turn rate. Use it with the attitude and coordination indications, and follow the aircraft procedure and instructor/operator guidance. Bank, load factor, and turn performance are connected; abrupt or excessive maneuvering can create hazards, and this calculator is a study aid rather than an instruction to fly a particular bank.

References and tools

The FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, Chapter 5, presents the relationship between bank angle, true airspeed, and rate of turn. Use the standard-rate bank calculator to compare the formula with the rule of thumb, and the rate-of-turn calculator to solve the reverse direction.
