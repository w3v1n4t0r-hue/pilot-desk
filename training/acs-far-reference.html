ACS and FAR study map

Use this page as a source map for your rating study. The Airman Certification Standards (ACS) describe the knowledge, risk-management, and skill elements evaluated on a practical test. The regulations, AIM, handbooks, and aircraft documents provide the detail behind those elements.

Check the current edition. FAA documents and regulations change. Confirm the revision date and use the current official publication before a checkride or flight.

Rating study checklist

  • Private Pilot: privileges and limitations, airworthiness, weather, airport operations, cross-country planning, performance, navigation, maneuvers, and emergency decisions.
  • Instrument Rating: IFR flight plans, weather and alternate decisions, instrument systems, clearances, holds, approaches, missed approaches, navigation failures, and risk management.
  • Commercial Pilot: commercial privileges, advanced performance, complex/high-performance systems as applicable, commercial maneuvers, and higher standards of risk management.
  • Flight Instructor: instructor responsibilities, learner-centered teaching, endorsements, knowledge transfer, scenario-based instruction, and demonstrating each task to the applicable standard.

How to study each ACS task

  1. Read the task objective and note every knowledge, risk-management, and skill element.
  2. Open the cited regulation, ACS reference, AIM paragraph, handbook chapter, or aircraft document.
  3. Write a short “legal, safe, and practical” explanation using a real aircraft and route.
  4. Practice the related calculation or scenario in PilotDesk, then verify the result against approved aircraft data.
  5. Ask your instructor to validate your interpretation and check the current ACS revision before scheduling the test.
