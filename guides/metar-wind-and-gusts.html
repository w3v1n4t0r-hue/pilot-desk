Reading METAR wind and gusts

The wind group near the beginning of a METAR gives direction and speed and may include gusts or variable direction. Understanding the group makes it much faster to estimate runway wind components.

Basic format

A group such as 24012KT means wind from 240 degrees at 12 knots. In U.S. METAR coding, the reported direction is true rather than the magnetic direction used in many tower wind reports and runway references.

Gusts

A group such as 24012G20KT means sustained wind 12 knots with gusts to 20 knots. The gust value is not an extra 20 knots; it is the reported peak gust speed.

Variable wind

VRB may appear when wind direction is variable under the reporting criteria. A separate directional variation group can also show the range through which the wind has varied.

Calm wind

00000KT indicates calm wind. Do not interpret the zeros as wind from north; the zero speed is the key.

Use the right direction reference

When comparing a METAR's true wind direction to a runway's magnetic direction, be aware that the references differ. Many weather interfaces handle that distinction when calculating runway components, but manual work should not silently mix true and magnetic directions.

Gusts and crosswind

A crosswind calculator can compute components for the sustained wind or gust value. Operational technique and limitations depend on the aircraft, runway and operator procedures, not just the arithmetic.
