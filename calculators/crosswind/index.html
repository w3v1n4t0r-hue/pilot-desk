Crosswind formula

Crosswind = Wind speed × sin(angle)
Headwind/Tailwind = Wind speed × cos(angle)

Worked example: runway 35, wind 320° at 18 kt

Runway 35 is approximately 350°. The wind angle is 30° left of the runway. Crosswind = 18 × sin 30° = 9 kt from the left; headwind = 18 × cos 30° ≈ 15.6 kt. The left crosswind pushes the aircraft to the right, so maintaining runway alignment requires correction into the wind. The calculator reports components only; compare the crosswind with the specific aircraft limitations and account for gusts and technique using approved aircraft guidance. See the crosswind components lesson.

For the default example—Runway 18, wind 220° at 20 knots—the relative angle is 40°. The crosswind is about 12.9 knots and the headwind component about 15.3 knots.

If a 20-knot wind is 30° off the runway, the crosswind is about 10 knots. At 60°, it is about 17 knots. Use these factors as a quick reasonableness check.

True vs. magnetic wind direction

Coded METAR winds are referenced to true north. Tower winds and ATIS or ASOS/AWOS radio voice winds are magnetic. Runway designators are magnetic references. When precision matters, keep the wind and runway direction references consistent.

Crosswind calculator questions

How do I calculate a gust crosswind?

Enter the steady wind first, record the answer, then replace wind speed with the reported gust speed and calculate again.

What does “from right” or “from left” mean?

It identifies which side of the aircraft/runway the crosswind is coming from based on the entered directions.

Is a demonstrated crosswind automatically a hard limit?

Not necessarily. Use the exact wording in the current POH/AFM and any operator limitations or personal minimums that apply.
