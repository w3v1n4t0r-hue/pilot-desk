Use one scenario across the whole lesson

Take a real airplane and a real route. Check the weather. Build the loading. Work the wind, time, fuel, and descent numbers. Brief the airport and procedures. Then change one thing: a stronger wind, a passenger, higher temperature, lower ceiling, failed alternator, or engine failure. The follow-up decision is usually where the useful studying happens.

For instructors

Use a PilotDesk page as the starting point for a question, not the answer sheet. Give the student the route, weather, loading, or performance problem first. Have them make an estimate and explain the decision, then use the calculator or guide to check the work. If the number is aircraft-specific, go back to the actual POH or AFM together.

What PilotDesk is not

It is not a replacement for the ACS, current regulations, the AIM, official weather, charts, NOTAMs, or the aircraft POH/AFM. The FAA does not publish its active knowledge-test question bank. PilotDesk identifies FAA-derived study items as paraphrases of published FAA sample questions and uses original FAA-aligned practice items for the rest. Start with the FAA ACS page and the current FAA aviation handbooks, then use PilotDesk alongside those sources.

Source review: September 2026. FAA standards and publications can change. Verify the current version before a knowledge test, practical test, or flight.
