Private Pilot ACS topics

The Private Pilot for Airplane Category ACS (FAA-S-ACS-6C) mixes knowledge, risk management, and flying skill. A useful study session should do the same. Pick a real route and airplane. Explain whether the flight is legal, whether it is smart, what the weather is doing, what the airplane can actually do, and what you would change if one part of the plan got worse.

Subjects to review

  • Airworthiness, required documents, inspections, equipment, and pilot privileges and limitations.
  • Weather products and the difference between reading a report and making a decision from it.
  • Cross-country planning: course, wind correction, groundspeed, time, fuel, alternates, airspace, and runway considerations.
  • Weight and balance and performance using the actual aircraft data, not generic numbers.
  • Aerodynamics, stalls, load factor, maneuvering speed, and the reasons behind the maneuver standards.
  • Emergency planning, diversion thinking, systems knowledge, and risk management.

Oral-review prompts

Practice answering these aloud: What makes the airplane airworthy today? What weather change would make you delay this trip? Which performance number in the POH worries you most on a hot day? If the alternator fails in cruise, what equipment do you still have and how long can you reasonably expect it to last? If your planned runway gets a stronger quartering tailwind, what changes?

FAA references

Find the current standard for your certificate on the FAA Airman Certification Standards page. Use relevant chapters from the FAA handbooks, the AIM, current regulations, and aircraft documents.
