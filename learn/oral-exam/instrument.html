Preflight Preparation · Cross-Country Flight Planning

When does an airplane IFR flight plan need an alternate?

Show answer

The exception requires an eligible instrument approach at the destination and appropriate weather information showing at least a 2,000-foot ceiling above airport elevation and 3 statute miles visibility throughout one hour before to one hour after ETA. If that exception is not met, I include an alternate. I then evaluate the alternate’s own forecast and applicable alternate minima.

Watch for

The destination test and alternate eligibility are separate. Do not apply 600–2 or 800–2 as destination landing minima.

Try this scenario

The destination meets the weather test, but no qualifying instrument approach is available. Does the weather alone remove the alternate requirement?

Discuss the decision

No. Both conditions of the exception must be satisfied.

Preflight Preparation · Cross-Country Flight Planning

Are standard alternate minima always 600–2 or 800–2?

Show answer

No. Use the alternate minima specified for the procedure and check restrictions and availability. When no different minima are specified, the airplane standards are 600 feet and 2 statute miles for a precision procedure, or 800 feet and 2 statute miles for a nonprecision procedure. With no instrument approach, forecast conditions must permit descent from the MEA, approach, and landing under basic VFR.

Watch for

A charted approach may be unavailable as an alternate. Equipment approvals and NOTAMs can also remove a seemingly suitable option.

Try this scenario

The weather is above standard minima, but a NOTAM removes the only usable approach. Is the airport still your planned alternate?

Discuss the decision

Reevaluate eligibility with the remaining procedures, equipment, and weather; select another alternate if you cannot meet the applicable requirements.

Preflight Preparation · Pilot Qualifications

I have six approaches logged. Does that prove I am instrument current?

Show answer

Not by itself. I check the dates, qualifying conditions and equipment, aircraft category, and all required tasks: approaches, holding, and intercepting and tracking courses. The usual lookback is the preceding six calendar months. If I do not meet the requirement, I cannot act as PIC under IFR or below VFR weather minimums merely because I am within the period available to regain currency without an IPC.

Watch for

The period available to regain currency is not permission to keep flying IFR as PIC while noncurrent.

Try this scenario

Your approaches are in date, but you cannot establish completion of the holding requirement. Can you accept an IFR clearance as PIC?

Discuss the decision

No. Establish compliance with the complete requirement before acting as PIC under IFR.

Instrument Approach Procedures · Precision and Nonprecision Approaches

What lets you descend below DA or MDA?

Show answer

For the usual Part 91 approach, the aircraft must continuously be in position to descend using normal maneuvers and a normal descent rate, flight visibility must meet the procedure’s requirement, and a listed visual reference must be distinctly visible and identifiable. Approach lights alone have a further restriction: below 100 feet above touchdown-zone elevation, the specified red terminating or side-row bars must also be visible, unless another qualifying reference is available.

Watch for

Seeing a light is not enough if the visibility or position requirement is not met. DA and MDA also demand different vertical procedures.

Try this scenario

You see the approach lights at DA, but the airplane is badly displaced from the final approach course. Do you continue?

Discuss the decision

No. The required position for a normal descent and landing is not satisfied; execute the missed approach.

Preflight Preparation · Cross-Country Flight Planning

What is the IFR airplane fuel requirement?

Show answer

Considering weather reports and forecasts, I need fuel to reach the first intended landing, then reach the alternate when required, and then fly 45 minutes at normal cruising speed. I also plan for realistic routing, climb, approach, missed approach, and delays. The regulation’s reserve is not my target for how little fuel I can have on landing.

Watch for

An alternate exemption does not eliminate the 45-minute requirement for an airplane.

Try this scenario

ATC offers a lengthy hold near the destination. How do you decide how much holding you can accept?

Discuss the decision

Work backward from the fuel needed for a safe diversion and landing with your reserve. Advise ATC early and leave before that margin is consumed.

Air Traffic Control Clearances and Procedures

What do you do with a clearance you cannot safely fly?

Show answer

I tell ATC that I am unable and request a workable alternative. I clarify uncertainty rather than guessing. Once a clearance is accepted, I comply unless I obtain an amendment, an emergency exists, or a qualifying collision-avoidance response requires a deviation. The PIC remains responsible for the operation.

Watch for

An ATC clearance does not waive aircraft limitations or make inadequate performance acceptable.

Try this scenario

A departure clearance requires a climb your loaded airplane cannot meet. What do you do before takeoff?

Discuss the decision

State unable and arrange another departure, runway, loading, timing, or route. Do not take off hoping the performance will improve.

Emergency Operations · Loss of Communications

What is your first regulatory decision after losing two-way radio communication?

Show answer

If the failure occurs in VFR conditions, or I encounter VFR conditions afterward, I continue under VFR and land as soon as practicable. If I must continue in IFR conditions, I apply the route, altitude, and clearance-limit provisions of 91.185 to the actual clearance and position. I troubleshoot without sacrificing aircraft control and use available communication alternatives.

Watch for

A route mnemonic does not answer the clearance-limit timing question. Read each part of the rule against your clearance.

Try this scenario

You lose communications in cloud, then reach sustained VFR conditions with a suitable airport nearby. Must you continue the filed route in IFR?

Discuss the decision

Apply the VFR provision: continue under VFR and land as soon as practicable rather than mechanically continuing the IFR route.

Preflight Procedures · Instrument and Equipment Check

Which inspection dates do you check before this IFR flight?

Show answer

I start with the aircraft’s general inspection and airworthiness requirements, then verify the checks applicable to the operation and equipment. Controlled-airspace IFR operations require the applicable altimeter and static-system checks. Transponder use has its own test requirements. If I use VOR navigation under IFR, I verify an eligible VOR check within the preceding 30 days and its record.

Watch for

The VOR check is tied to using that navigation system under IFR; do not confuse it with the longer inspection intervals for other equipment.

Try this scenario

The VOR check is out of date and your proposed route relies on VOR navigation. What must change?

Discuss the decision

Complete and record a compliant check, or establish a different legal and operationally suitable navigation plan using approved equipment.
