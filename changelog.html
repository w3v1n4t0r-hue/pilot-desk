What’s New

September 2026

PilotDesk now behaves more like a pilot workspace than a collection of separate calculator pages. Calculator inputs can be preserved in shareable URLs, results can be copied, shared, printed, saved in local history, and reopened later. Favorites, recently used tools, saved scenarios, and command search make frequently used tools much faster to reach.

A new Flight Planning Workspace connects wind correction, groundspeed, enroute time, fuel, top of descent, and descent rate in one flow. The individual calculators remain available for focused work, and the workspace links back to them so the math can be checked one step at a time.

Calculator pages now provide stronger related-tool paths, formula/reference links, worked-example controls, result context, and direct embed options on supported calculators. The homepage also highlights popular tools such as the Online E6B, Pilot Math Formula Reference, Crosswind Calculator, Density Altitude Calculator, and the new planning workspace.

Mobile use was expanded with a bottom quick-navigation bar, clearer result handling, and a more complete installable web-app experience. Core pilot-math pages are cached for offline use after they have been installed or cached by the browser. Live weather still requires a network connection and is never represented as current when offline.

Major PilotDesk pages now have dedicated sharing-card artwork, and the site branding has been centralized around one logo asset. That means a future PilotDesk logo can be swapped across the header, web-app icon, and structured branding without rewriting every page.

Core calculator pages continue to include formulas, assumptions, examples, source guidance, sanity checks, and aviation-safety context. PilotDesk is not FAA approved and is a supplemental educational and planning aid; operational decisions must use current approved aircraft data, charts, weather, NOTAMs, procedures, and regulations as applicable.
