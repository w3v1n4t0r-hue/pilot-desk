How to calculate crosswind component

A reported wind almost never lines up perfectly with the runway. The useful question is how much of that wind acts down the runway and how much acts across it.

The basic math

Find the smallest angle between the runway heading and the direction the wind is coming from. Then:

Crosswind = wind speed × sin(angle)
Headwind = wind speed × cos(angle)

If the headwind result is negative, that portion is a tailwind. The side of the crosswind depends on whether the wind is coming from the left or right of the runway heading.

Example

Runway 18 is approximately 180°. With wind from 220° at 20 knots, the angle is 40°. The crosswind is about 12.9 knots and the headwind is about 15.3 knots.

Gusts matter

If the METAR includes a gust, calculate the component again using the gust speed. That gives you the higher crosswind and headwind/tailwind component that may occur during the gust.

Runway numbers are rounded

A runway labeled 18 is not guaranteed to be exactly 180°. Published runway magnetic headings can differ, and magnetic variation changes over time. For operational use, verify the current airport/runway data rather than assuming the painted number is exact.

Quick mental checks

You can estimate a crosswind before reaching for a calculator. At 30 degrees off the runway, the crosswind is about half the wind speed. At 45 degrees it is about 70 percent, at 60 degrees about 87 percent, and at 90 degrees the full wind speed is crosswind. These are useful reasonableness checks, not a replacement for using the actual wind and runway values when the number matters.

Which runway gives the lower crosswind?

If more than one runway is available, compare the relative wind angle for each runway before doing the full math. The runway pointed more nearly into the wind will usually have the smaller crosswind and larger headwind component. Runway availability, traffic flow, runway condition, aircraft limits, and ATC instructions still matter, so the smallest calculated crosswind is not automatically the runway you will use.
