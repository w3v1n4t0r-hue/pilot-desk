Preflight Preparation · Airworthiness Requirements

The landing light is broken. Can we fly?

Show answer

I need to know the aircraft, the operation, and which equipment rules apply before deciding. If we use an approved MEL, I follow its authorization and conditions. Otherwise, I first check that the aircraft and operation qualify for 91.213(d). I check type-certification requirements, the equipment list or KOEL, operating rules, and applicable airworthiness directives. If the item is not required, it still needs proper removal or deactivation, placarding, and any required maintenance record. An appropriately qualified pilot or maintenance person must determine that it presents no hazard.

Watch for

A placard alone does not make a required item optional. A rental airplane is not automatically an operation for hire for every equipment rule.

Try this scenario

The same failure is discovered before a night flight. What additional equipment requirement and practical risk would you check?

Discuss the decision

Check the applicable night equipment requirements and the nature of the operation. Even if dispatch is legally permissible, consider landing, taxi, obstacle detection, and your personal limits.

Preflight Preparation · Pilot Qualifications

Do three daytime landings make me current to carry someone tonight?

Show answer

They do not establish the special night landing experience. For the period from one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise, the general rule requires three takeoffs and three full-stop landings in the preceding 90 days, during that same night window, as sole manipulator, in the appropriate category, class, and type when required. I also check the general recent-experience rule and any applicable exceptions.

Watch for

Logging night time and meeting night passenger-carrying experience use different definitions. Do not use one clock for both.

Try this scenario

Sunset is 1900. You made three full-stop landings at 1930. Do those establish the required night experience?

Discuss the decision

No. Those landings occurred before the special recent-experience window began at 2000.

Preflight Preparation · Cross-Country Flight Planning

What fuel must we have before starting this VFR airplane flight?

Show answer

Considering forecast wind and weather, I need enough to reach the first intended landing and then fly at normal cruise for at least 30 minutes by day or 45 minutes at night. That is a legal minimum for beginning the flight. My plan also accounts for taxi, climb, expected delays, usable fuel, uncertainty, and an earlier diversion point.

Watch for

Do not confuse fuel capacity with usable fuel, or plan to arrive with an empty reserve because the flight was legal at departure.

Try this scenario

The headwind is stronger than forecast and your landing estimate is approaching your personal reserve. What changes?

Discuss the decision

Recompute endurance from actual performance, select a suitable nearer airport, and act before the remaining options become marginal.

Preflight Preparation · Airworthiness Requirements

Where do you establish that this airplane can be flown today?

Show answer

I check the required certificates aboard, the operating limitations and approved flight-manual material, and the aircraft records for inspections and applicable airworthiness directives. Then I inspect its present condition. A current inspection entry does not excuse a defect found on preflight; the PIC must determine that the aircraft is in condition for safe flight.

Watch for

The registration and airworthiness certificates do not establish that every inspection, directive, limitation, or current defect is acceptable.

Try this scenario

The annual is current, but you find a damaged tire. Is the annual enough to dispatch?

Discuss the decision

No. Evaluate the present condition against the aircraft requirements and obtain qualified maintenance assistance when needed.

Preflight Preparation · Weather Information

Is checking the destination METAR a complete briefing?

Show answer

No. I need the information relevant to the whole flight. For a flight away from the airport vicinity, that includes weather reports and forecasts, fuel requirements, alternatives if the flight cannot be completed, and known ATC delays. I also need runway lengths and applicable takeoff and landing performance information. I consider route terrain, airspace, notices, and how conditions may change before arrival.

Watch for

A VFR observation at one airport describes neither the weather along the route nor the conditions at your arrival time.

Try this scenario

Both airports report VFR, but low clouds cover the ridge between them. What is your decision?

Discuss the decision

Assess terrain clearance, cloud clearance, escape options, and a route that can remain legal and safe. Delay or reroute if those cannot be established.

Preflight Preparation · Performance and Limitations

If we are below maximum weight, are we within limits?

Show answer

Not necessarily. Weight and center of gravity must both remain within the approved envelope. I use the actual aircraft loading information, usable fuel, occupants, baggage, and moment method. I check the conditions that matter during the flight, including fuel burn. Performance calculations are a separate check: a legal loading can still leave inadequate runway or climb margin.

Watch for

A generic aircraft profile does not replace the current weight-and-balance record for the particular airplane.

Try this scenario

Removing rear baggage brings the CG forward. What else changes in your planning?

Discuss the decision

Recalculate weight and CG, then use the new loading in applicable performance calculations. Do not assume that one correction resolves every limit.

Preflight Preparation · National Airspace System

Does talking to ATC always authorize entry into controlled airspace?

Show answer

No. I identify the airspace class and its actual entry requirements. Class B normally requires an explicit clearance before entry. Class C and D require the specified two-way communications before entry and while within the airspace. Aircraft equipment, pilot qualifications, weather, and any restrictions also matter.

Watch for

A radio response is not a Class B clearance. Do not apply one airspace class’s communication rule to another.

Try this scenario

Approaching Class B, ATC says your call sign and tells you to stand by. Can you enter?

Discuss the decision

Remain outside until you receive the required clearance. Plan the hold or diversion before reaching the boundary.

Emergency Operations · Emergency Approach and Landing

Can the PIC deviate from a rule in an emergency?

Show answer

In an in-flight emergency requiring immediate action, the PIC may deviate from a Part 91 rule to the extent required to meet that emergency. I first control the airplane and take the action needed for safety, then communicate when able. If the FAA requests it, a written report of the deviation is required.

Watch for

Emergency authority is limited to what the emergency requires; it is not advance permission to disregard planning or limitations.

Try this scenario

An engine failure leaves a suitable landing area inside otherwise restricted airspace. What takes priority?

Discuss the decision

Maintain control and execute the safest achievable landing. Use emergency authority to the extent necessary, and communicate your situation when workload permits.
