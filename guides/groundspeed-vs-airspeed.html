Groundspeed versus airspeed

An airplane can have several different speed values at the same moment. Indicated airspeed, true airspeed and groundspeed answer different questions, so using the correct one is essential for performance and navigation.

Indicated airspeed

IAS is the speed shown on the airspeed indicator before corrections for instrument, position, compressibility or density effects. Aircraft operating limitations and many maneuver speeds are based on indicated or calibrated airspeed.

True airspeed

TAS is the aircraft's speed through the surrounding air mass. As air density decreases with altitude, true airspeed is generally higher than indicated airspeed for the same dynamic pressure.

Groundspeed

Groundspeed is the aircraft's speed relative to the ground. Wind changes it. A 20-knot headwind reduces groundspeed by roughly 20 knots when aligned with the route, while a 20-knot tailwind increases it by roughly the same amount.

Why ETA uses groundspeed

Arrival time depends on how quickly the aircraft moves over Earth, so groundspeed belongs in time-distance calculations. Using TAS in place of groundspeed ignores the wind effect.

Why aerodynamic calculations use airspeed

Stall, maneuvering and aerodynamic performance relationships depend on motion through the air, not motion over the ground. A strong headwind can make groundspeed low while the airplane still has normal airspeed.

Crosswind makes the relationship two-dimensional

When wind is not directly along the route, the wind triangle determines both a wind-correction angle and groundspeed. Simply adding or subtracting the full wind speed is only appropriate for a direct headwind or tailwind.
