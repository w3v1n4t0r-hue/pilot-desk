How to use the aircraft glide distance calculator

aircraft glide distance: Enter the height available above the landing surface and a glide ratio appropriate to the training example. The calculator returns the simple still-air horizontal distance in nautical miles, feet and statute miles.

For aircraft-specific work, take glide speed, configuration and performance information from the current POH or AFM. Do not substitute a generic ratio from another aircraft.

Formula and method: glide distance

Horizontal distance in feet = height available in feet × glide ratio. Nautical miles are found by dividing horizontal feet by about 6,076.12.

With the default example, 5,000 feet × 9 gives 45,000 feet. That is about 7.4 NM or 8.5 statute miles in the simplified no-wind geometry.

What the result means

the primary output: The number is a geometric reference, not a guaranteed reachable radius. A headwind reduces ground range. Turns use altitude. Terrain may sit above the assumed landing surface. Gear, flaps, a windmilling propeller, ice, damage and other drag-producing conditions can reduce performance.

Common glide-calculation mistakes

Use height above the intended landing surface rather than blindly using MSL altitude. Keep the ratio and distance units consistent. Do not assume best glide speed and glide ratio are the same quantity, and do not assume a still-air answer includes the effect of wind.

Glide distance questions

How far can an airplane glide from 5,000 feet?

It depends on the aircraft and the usable height above the landing surface. With a 9:1 training ratio, 5,000 feet gives about 45,000 feet or 7.4 NM in still air.

Does wind change glide distance?

Wind changes the distance traveled over the ground during the descent. A headwind reduces ground range and a tailwind increases it, so a still-air result should not be treated as a precise reachable boundary.

Is best glide speed the same as glide ratio?

No. Best glide speed is an airspeed for the aircraft under stated conditions; glide ratio is the relationship between horizontal distance and altitude lost.

Can I use this calculator as the only source in an emergency?

No. Follow the current aircraft POH/AFM, approved checklist and trained procedures. PilotDesk is a supplemental training and calculation aid.
