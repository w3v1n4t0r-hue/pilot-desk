Mach number and the speed of sound

Mach number compares an aircraft's true airspeed with the local speed of sound. Mach 1 means the aircraft is traveling at the local speed of sound; Mach 0.80 means it is traveling at 80 percent of that speed.

The speed of sound is not fixed

In the atmosphere, local speed of sound depends mainly on air temperature. Colder air has a lower speed of sound than warmer air. That is why one fixed knots value cannot represent Mach 1 at every altitude and condition.

Mach formula

Mach number = true airspeed ÷ local speed of sound, using matching units. If the local sound speed is 600 knots and true airspeed is 450 knots, the result is Mach 0.75.

Why altitude is related indirectly

Altitude itself is not the direct input controlling sound speed; temperature is. In the standard atmosphere, temperature generally decreases with altitude through the troposphere, which makes altitude and sound speed appear related.

Operational use

High-speed aircraft use Mach limits because compressibility effects become important before reaching Mach 1. Aircraft-specific limitations and indicated Mach values must come from approved instruments and documentation.
