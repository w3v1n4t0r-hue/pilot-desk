Standard-Rate Turns

A standard-rate turn changes heading at 3° per second. At that rate, a 360° turn takes two minutes. The bank angle required increases as true airspeed increases.

The quick pilot estimate

Bank angle ≈ 15% of TAS

At 120 kt TAS, that quick estimate gives about 18° of bank. PilotDesk also calculates the bank angle from the actual coordinated-turn relationship for a 3°/second target.

Why TAS matters

Faster airplanes cover more distance each second, so they require more bank to achieve the same turn rate. This is why one bank angle does not produce a standard-rate turn at every speed.

Use it correctly

This is useful for instrument-training math and understanding turn geometry. Fly the airplane using its instruments, procedures, limitations and instructor/operator guidance rather than chasing a calculator result in flight.

What changes with airspeed

A standard-rate turn means roughly 3 degrees of heading change per second, so a full 360-degree turn takes about two minutes. At a higher true airspeed the airplane covers more distance each second, which means it needs more bank to keep the same turn rate. That is why a single memorized bank angle does not work at every speed.

Rule of thumb versus exact bank

Training shortcuts are useful for setting an initial bank angle, while the turn coordinator or other approved indication tells you whether the actual turn rate is on target. The calculator lets you compare the shortcut with the geometric result rather than treating either as an aircraft limitation.
