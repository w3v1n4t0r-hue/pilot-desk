Holding pattern leg distance

Traditional holding patterns often use time to define the inbound leg. Knowing groundspeed lets you estimate how much distance the airplane covers during that timed segment.

Distance from time and groundspeed

Distance in nautical miles = groundspeed in knots × time in minutes ÷ 60. At 120 knots, a one-minute leg covers about 2 NM in still-air groundspeed terms.

Wind changes the picture

Holding procedures are flown relative to the fix and protected airspace, not simply by trying to cover a fixed distance. Wind changes outbound timing, inbound groundspeed, drift correction, and the shape of the pattern.

Why the estimate is useful

Distance estimates help pilots build intuition for how far an airplane moves during a hold and can be useful for training, fuel planning, or situational awareness.

Do not replace procedure guidance

Actual holding entries, timing, speeds, leg lengths, and corrections must follow current ATC clearances, procedures, regulations, and instrument guidance.
