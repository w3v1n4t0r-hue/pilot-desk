How this density altitude calculator works

PilotDesk derives dry-air density from pressure altitude and temperature using a standard-atmosphere model, then converts the result to the equivalent ISA altitude. This is more detailed than the common classroom shortcut.

Quick density altitude formula

DA ≈ PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA temperature)

The 120-feet-per-degree formula is a useful mental approximation. At 5,000 feet pressure altitude with an OAT of 30°C, ISA temperature is about 5°C, so the shortcut gives roughly 8,000 feet density altitude.

What high density altitude means

High density altitude means lower air density. Depending on the aircraft, that can mean reduced engine and propeller performance, longer takeoff distance, and reduced climb capability. Always continue from the atmospheric calculation into the aircraft's approved performance data.

Pressure altitude vs density altitude

Pressure altitude is the standard-atmosphere altitude corresponding to pressure. Density altitude adjusts the density reference for nonstandard conditions—especially temperature. On a hot day, density altitude can be far above pressure altitude.

Density altitude questions

What do I enter for pressure altitude?

Use pressure altitude, not simply field elevation. If needed, calculate it from field elevation and altimeter setting first.

Does this include humidity?

This calculator uses a dry-air ISA model. Humidity can reduce air density, but aircraft-specific operational performance should be determined using the method in the current approved aircraft data.

Can density altitude be below field elevation?

Yes. Cold, dense air can produce a density altitude lower than the physical field elevation.
