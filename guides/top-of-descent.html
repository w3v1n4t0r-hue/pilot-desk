FLIGHT PLANNING

Top of Descent

A top-of-descent estimate answers a simple question: how far from the target should the descent begin to lose the planned altitude at the chosen gradient?

Distance from gradient

Descent distance = altitude to lose ÷ descent gradient

Losing 6,000 ft at 300 ft/NM requires about 20 NM. At 120 kt groundspeed, 300 ft/NM corresponds to about 600 fpm.

The common 3-to-1 shortcut

For a roughly three-degree descent, pilots often estimate about 3 NM for every 1,000 ft to lose, then adjust for wind, restrictions and aircraft energy management.

What the math does not know

A simple calculation does not account for ATC restrictions, published procedures, terrain, speed changes, wind changes, icing, aircraft configuration or operator procedures. Treat it as planning math, not an instruction to descend.
