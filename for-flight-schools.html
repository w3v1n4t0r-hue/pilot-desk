Why direct links are useful

Calculator URLs can keep the entered values, so an instructor can send the exact setup used in a ground lesson. Students can change one variable, recalculate, and explain why the answer moved. The formula and assumptions stay visible, which makes it harder to turn the calculator into a black box.

Use Daily for repeat traffic and repetition

PilotDesk Daily gives the whole class the same three-question challenge each day. It works without an account, so a CFI can drop one link into a group chat or lesson page. Students who want to keep XP and streaks can sign in, but the quiz itself stays open.

Link because it helps, not because of SEO

PilotDesk does not require a reciprocal link to use public tools or widgets. If a school, instructor, or club independently decides a PilotDesk page belongs on its resource page, link to the page that is useful to the student. That is the entire ask. No paid ranking link, no forced anchor text, and no link exchange.

What to verify before teaching from a page

Use current approved sources: the FAA standard, regulation, handbook, chart, official weather source, and exact aircraft POH/AFM as the controlling material. PilotDesk publishes its sources and methods, includes correction links, and keeps aircraft-specific numbers out of generic articles when one universal number would be misleading.

Training aid only. A school or instructor remains responsible for the sources, syllabus, aircraft data, and procedures used in actual training and flight operations.
