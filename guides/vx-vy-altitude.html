Vx vs Vy and Altitude

Vx and Vy are two common aircraft-performance terms students encounter during ground school and checkride preparation. Vx describes best angle of climb, while Vy describes best rate of climb.

Vx in plain English

Best angle compares altitude gained with horizontal distance traveled. That makes Vx a distance-based performance concept.

Vy in plain English

Best rate compares altitude gained with elapsed time. That makes Vy a time-based performance concept.

How altitude changes the relationship

For a typical normally aspirated airplane, indicated Vx generally trends upward with altitude while indicated Vy trends downward. The two values move closer together as available climb performance decreases.

A useful way to remember it

Think of Vx as the distance question and Vy as the time question. This distinction is often more useful for studying than memorizing the letters without understanding what they measure.

Why performance charts still matter

Aircraft models do not all use identical values. Weight, configuration and atmospheric conditions also affect performance, so students should connect the concept to the aircraft-specific material used in their training.
