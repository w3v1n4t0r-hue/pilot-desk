Percent mean aerodynamic chord explained

Some aircraft express center-of-gravity position as a percentage of mean aerodynamic chord, or %MAC, instead of only an arm measured from a datum. The percentage shows where the CG lies between the leading and trailing edges of the reference chord.

The formula

%MAC = ((CG arm − LEMAC) ÷ MAC length) × 100. LEMAC is the leading edge of the mean aerodynamic chord measured from the same datum used for the CG arm.

Worked example

Assume the calculated CG arm is 85 inches, LEMAC is 70 inches, and MAC length is 60 inches. The CG is 15 inches aft of LEMAC. Fifteen divided by 60 is 0.25, so the CG is at 25% MAC.

Why the same datum matters

CG arm and LEMAC must use the same reference datum. Mixing values from different reference systems makes the result meaningless even if the arithmetic is correct.

What percent MAC tells you

A lower percentage is farther forward on the chord; a higher percentage is farther aft. Aircraft documents may publish an allowable CG envelope in %MAC, arm, moment or a combination of formats.

Do not invent the limits

The calculator can convert a known CG location into %MAC, but it cannot determine whether the loading is legal or safe without the aircraft's approved CG limits for the actual weight and configuration.
