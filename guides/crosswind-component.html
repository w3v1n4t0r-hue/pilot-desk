How to Calculate Crosswind Component

A wind report can be split into two useful parts: the crosswind component acting across the runway and the headwind or tailwind component acting along it. PilotDesk calculates both from runway direction, wind direction, and wind speed.

Open Crosswind CalculatorCrosswind Component Chart

Crosswind component formula

First find the smallest angle between the runway direction and the direction the wind is coming from. Then use:

Crosswind = Wind speed × sin(relative angle)
Headwind/Tailwind = Wind speed × cos(relative angle)

The sign and geometry tell you whether the crosswind is from the left or right and whether the along-runway component is a headwind or tailwind.

Worked crosswind example

Suppose Runway 18 is treated as 180° and the wind is from 220° at 20 knots. The relative angle is 40°. Crosswind is 20 × sin(40°), or about 12.9 knots. The headwind component is 20 × cos(40°), or about 15.3 knots.

If the wind were instead from 140° at 20 knots, the crosswind magnitude would still be about 12.9 knots, but it would come from the opposite side.

Quick crosswind mental-math chart

At 30°, use about one-half of the wind for crosswind. At 45°, use about seven-tenths. At 60°, nearly nine-tenths. At 90°, all of the wind is crosswind. These are excellent reasonableness checks when using a calculator or FAA component chart.

How to calculate crosswind with gusts

If the wind is reported as 220° at 15 gusting 25 knots, calculate the component at 15 knots and then run the same angle again at 25 knots. With a 40° relative angle, the steady crosswind is about 9.6 knots while the gust crosswind is about 16.1 knots.

PilotDesk's calculator accepts one wind speed at a time, so simply run the calculation again using the gust value. This makes the steady and gust cases easy to compare rather than hiding the stronger component inside one number.

METAR winds vs. tower and ATIS winds

Make sure your direction references match. In U.S. aviation weather products, the coded METAR wind direction is referenced to true north. ATIS and ASOS/AWOS radio voice winds are reported relative to magnetic north, and tower-issued winds are magnetic as well. Runway numbers and runway magnetic direction are magnetic references.

For a quick training calculation, using the displayed runway number may be close enough to learn the geometry. When the result matters operationally, use current runway data and consistent true/magnetic references. See the FAA Aeronautical Information Manual weather guidance.

Runway numbers are rounded

Runway 18 does not necessarily point exactly 180°. Runway designators are rounded from magnetic orientation, and published runway information can provide a more precise direction. If the wind component is close to an aircraft, operator, or personal limit, use the current runway data instead of assuming the painted number is exact.

Which runway has the lowest crosswind?

The runway most nearly aligned into the wind usually produces the smallest crosswind and largest headwind component. But crosswind magnitude is not the only runway-selection factor. Runway length, surface, slope, traffic flow, obstacles, NOTAMs, aircraft performance, ATC instructions, and a possible tailwind all matter.

Demonstrated crosswind vs. a limitation

Do not automatically treat every POH “maximum demonstrated crosswind” value as a hard limitation. The wording and certification basis matter. Read the current POH/AFM and follow any actual aircraft or operator limitation. Personal minimums can of course be more restrictive than either.

Crosswind questions

How do you calculate crosswind component?

Multiply wind speed by the sine of the angle between the runway direction and the wind direction.

How do you calculate headwind or tailwind?

Multiply wind speed by the cosine of the relative angle. If the wind is more than 90° from the runway heading, the along-runway component is a tailwind.

What is the crosswind at 30 degrees?

At 30° off the runway, crosswind is exactly one-half the wind speed because sin(30°) = 0.5.

Do gusts change the crosswind component?

Yes. If direction stays the same and speed increases in the gust, both crosswind and along-runway components increase proportionally.
