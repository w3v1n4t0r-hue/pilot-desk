How to use the Dynamic Hydroplaning Speed calculator

Enter main-tire pressure to evaluate the FAA dynamic hydroplaning speed estimate. The output is a condition worth understanding, not a target speed.

Inputs

  • Tire pressure — enter the value in psi.

Formula and method

FAA dynamic hydroplaning speed estimate ≈ 8.6 × √main tire pressure in psi. The commonly rounded 9 × √psi rule is a different approximation; neither establishes a safe speed or stopping distance.

What the result means

The estimate concerns dynamic hydroplaning. Other forms can occur under different conditions, and this formula does not predict braking or stopping distance.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use the result as a safe landing speed. Runway reports, aircraft guidance, contamination, and braking action remain central.

The FAA Airplane Flying Handbook gives the dynamic estimate as 8.6 × √(main-tire pressure in PSI). At 36 PSI, that is 51.6 knots. This estimate does not predict a safe speed or stopping distance; viscous hydroplaning can occur below it. See the FAA Airplane Flying Handbook, Chapter 9.
