Aircraft power loading explained

Power loading compares aircraft weight with available engine horsepower. It is usually expressed as pounds per horsepower and gives a simple way to compare how much aircraft mass each unit of engine power has to move.

Power loading formula

Power loading = aircraft weight ÷ horsepower. A 2,400-pound airplane with a 180-horsepower engine has a power loading of about 13.3 pounds per horsepower.

Lower versus higher numbers

All else equal, a lower power-loading number means more power is available for each pound of aircraft. That can support stronger acceleration and climb performance. But propeller efficiency, drag, altitude, temperature, engine condition, and configuration all matter.

Weight changes power loading

Because the formula uses actual aircraft weight, loading the airplane more heavily raises the pounds-per-horsepower value. Burning fuel lowers it over time.

Use it as a comparison tool

Power loading is useful for comparing designs or understanding why weight affects performance, but it cannot predict a specific takeoff roll or climb rate by itself.
